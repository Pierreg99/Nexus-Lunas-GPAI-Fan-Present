# Freebie roadmap

This is a decision aid for improving a free gift over time, not a schedule or a promise to recipients. Advance work only when the current stage is useful, supportable, and consistent with `RULES.md`. Dates are relative horizons; re-estimate before committing.

## Horizon 1 — Make the current gift dependable (now–2 weeks)

**Goal:** a first-time recipient can get the promised value from the files already delivered.

Candidate work:

- Fix verified broken links, confusing first-run instructions, and high-severity accessibility or privacy problems.
- Test the downloaded archive on a fresh device/browser profile and confirm it contains only the intended files.
- Add a plain-text path for critical instructions; clearly disclose local data behavior and unsupported features.
- Invite a small number of people to try one core task, only with consent and without requiring public feedback.

**Exit evidence:** the core path passes the brief's checks; any remaining limits are documented; no blocker remains. If no one has tried it, say “not yet tested with recipients.”

## Horizon 2 — Improve from observed friction (2–6 weeks)

**Goal:** make one evidence-backed improvement that reduces a real recipient's effort.

Candidate work, subject to feedback:

- Clarify steps or terms where a tester paused.
- Add one small worked example for a prompt or starter-project change that caused confusion.
- Improve keyboard, mobile, contrast, or assistive-technology behavior based on an observed issue.
- Offer a second format (for example, printable text alongside a browser tool) only if people need it and it can be maintained.
- Add a translation only when a fluent reviewer can check meaning, tone, and instructions.

**Decision gate:** compare recipient benefit, build effort, maintenance burden, privacy impact, and evidence quality. Keep no more than one or two changes in progress. Decline changes that merely add novelty or tracking.

## Horizon 3 — Extend the collection carefully (6–12 weeks)

**Goal:** explore a second, distinct gift only after the existing one is understood and supportable.

Possible experiments:

- A new standalone mini-project or themed prompt set, with its own audience, brief, and acceptance checks.
- An optional printable or offline alternative for a specific access need.
- A contributor checklist or asset-permission record if community contributions are actually expected.
- A small index that helps people choose a gift without requiring an account or collecting a profile.

**Decision gate:** approve a separate brief and realistic owner/time budget. Do not create a membership, mailing-list wall, paywall, analytics pipeline, or public roadmap commitment as a side effect of a freebie.

## Backlog and prioritization

For each candidate, record the observed evidence, recipient task affected, benefit, effort, ongoing owner, rights/access/privacy risks, and a simple acceptance check. Prefer, in order:

1. A confirmed blocker or a misleading promise.
2. A repeated, concrete first-use problem.
3. An accessibility or privacy improvement with a clear remedy.
4. A small optional enhancement with a named maintainer.

Put ideas with no evidence or owner in “later / do not promise.” A download count alone does not show that the gift was useful. Avoid hidden tracking; if feedback is collected, keep it optional, minimal, and transparent.

## What never becomes a roadmap shortcut

No feature ships without checking its full cost, data behavior, accessibility, third-party rights, and support needs. A future possibility is not a current benefit. Tell recipients what exists today, and update the brief and instructions before a changed promise is shared.
