# Recipient profiles

These are starting hypotheses for designing a free gift, not research findings or claims about every fan. Confirm them with real, permission-based feedback before using demographic assumptions or making a strong promise. Edit or replace a profile whenever the evidence says to.

## 1. The first-time maker

**Situation:** Has an idea and curiosity, but has not finished a small digital project before. May use a phone, a shared device, or a computer with limited setup permissions.

**Job to help with:** Choose one attainable next step and understand how a tiny example works.

**What success looks like:** They can open the resource, complete one small action, and see what to try next without creating an account or installing a large toolchain.

**Likely friction to check:** Unfamiliar vocabulary, unclear file paths, too many choices, setup assumptions, or fear that a mistake will break something.

**Design implications:** Put exact open/use instructions first; keep version one tiny; explain terms in context; give a worked example and a safe way to experiment; mention browser and storage limits.

## 2. The community gift curator

**Situation:** Wants to offer a thoughtful downloadable resource to a fan or creator community, with limited time and no dedicated product team.

**Job to help with:** Make the gift easy to understand, access, maintain, and share without promising a whole platform.

**What success looks like:** A recipient knows what is included, how to use it, what it does not do, and where to go if something is unclear.

**Likely friction to check:** Unclear audience fit, unauthorized fan artwork or third-party content, inaccessible file formats, broken links, and a package that depends on the creator staying online.

**Design implications:** Keep an allow-listed file manifest, attribution and permission notes, a plain-text fallback, stable local instructions, and an extracted-archive test. Do not imply endorsement by an artist, community, or platform without permission.

## 3. The early supporter

**Situation:** Chose to engage while an idea is still taking shape; appreciates a sincere thank-you more than an elaborate loyalty scheme.

**Job to help with:** Feel welcomed and receive something complete and useful now, not merely a promise of future rewards.

**What success looks like:** The gift stands on its own, communicates gratitude without pressure, and can be kept or used without sharing personal information.

**Likely friction to check:** A thank-you that feels generic, a name field that is required, pressure to post or recruit, hidden future costs, or an unclear distinction between a finished gift and a planned feature.

**Design implications:** Make personalization optional; keep the free value intact; never condition the gift on a testimonial, referral, or mailing-list sign-up; be transparent about future plans.

## Choose and validate

Before using a profile, record:

1. Which profile best fits this specific gift—and why?
2. What is known from direct observation or supplied evidence?
3. What is still a hypothesis?
4. What single, low-risk conversation or task test could correct the hypothesis?

Do not collect sensitive information just to make a profile sound realistic. A clear task and constraint are more useful than invented demographic detail.
