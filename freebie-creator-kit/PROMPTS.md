# Original prompts for making better freebies

Copy one prompt into the AI assistant you choose. Replace every `[bracket]` with facts you can stand behind. If you do not know a detail, write “unknown—ask me”; never let the model fill a gap with invented research. Examples and concepts produced by an AI need human review.

## 1. Write a one-page freebie brief

```text
Act as a careful freebie product strategist. Help me define one useful, genuinely free resource.

What I know:
- Topic or idea: [idea]
- Intended audience: [who, based on what evidence]
- Problem they have: [specific situation]
- Time and skills available to make it: [constraints]
- Existing materials I can reuse: [materials and rights/permissions]
- Where it may be shared: [known channel or “not decided”]

Create a one-page brief with: audience and situation; the single job to help with; promised outcome; deliverable and format; scope and explicit exclusions; evidence versus assumptions; accessibility and privacy needs; risks and dependencies; and three observable acceptance checks. Ask up to three focused questions first if a missing fact changes the recommendation. Keep the first version small. Do not invent research, testimonials, legal rights, platform features, prices, or outcomes. Apply the attached Freebie Creator Kit rules: [paste RULES.md or its relevant points].
```

## 2. Test whether the idea helps someone

```text
Act as a skeptical, kind discovery partner for this proposed freebie: [idea and brief]. Separate provided evidence from assumptions. Name the audience's likely existing workaround, the strongest reason this might not help, and the smallest ethical test I can run without collecting sensitive data or building the whole product. Draft a neutral invitation and up to five open questions that do not lead people toward praise. Give a decision rule for proceed / revise / pause. Do not claim a market size, demand, conversion rate, or user preference without supplied evidence. Label all hypotheses clearly.
```

## 3. Choose a format and cut version one

```text
Given this verified need and these constraints: [paste], compare at most three deliverable formats (for example, a checklist, editable template, short guide, or offline mini-tool). For each, show who it fits, effort, accessibility, privacy, maintenance, and likely failure modes. Recommend the smallest format that can produce this one outcome: [outcome]. List must-have content, a definition of done, and ideas deliberately deferred. Prefer no account, no required API key, no unnecessary tracking, and no recurring service. State any assumption that could change the recommendation.
```

## 4. Write copy-ready prompts for the freebie

```text
Create [number] original, practical prompts for a free resource about [topic] for [audience]. Each prompt must have a distinct job, clear bracketed inputs, constraints against made-up facts, and a concrete output shape. Include one clearly labeled illustrative input and response per prompt; do not present examples as real user results or live AI output. Use plain, inclusive language. Avoid asking for passwords, private keys, sensitive personal data, or confidential material. Include a reminder to verify consequential facts and review the chosen AI tool's privacy settings. Flag any prompt that depends on current information.
```

## 5. Build the smallest usable resource

```text
Act as a beginner-friendly maker. Implement or draft version one of this approved brief: [paste]. Deliverable: [format]. Constraints: [offline/online, tools, time, audience, accessibility]. Provide complete content or files, exact open/use instructions, and a concise explanation of how a recipient completes the core task. Keep the promised outcome modest and testable. Make controls keyboard-usable and labeled; use plain language; handle empty, invalid, and long input; use safe text rendering for user input; avoid external calls and tracking unless separately approved. Do not add accounts, analytics, payments, sign-up gates, hidden upsells, or unrequested features. State what you could not verify.
```

## 6. Turn an approved brief into a work plan

```text
Plan this approved freebie: [brief]. Available effort and people: [facts]. Break it into at most seven ordered tasks, each with an owner role, rough effort, dependency, concrete output, and testable done condition. Identify the riskiest assumption and test it before expensive work. Include content review, accessibility, privacy, rights/attribution, packaging, and extracted-archive checks where relevant. Mark estimates as estimates. Finish with a release gate and a short “not in version one” list; do not quietly expand scope.
```

## 7. Review it as a recipient and release gate

```text
Review this freebie against its audience profile, brief, and rules: [paste or attach]. Inspect promise-versus-reality, first-use clarity, missing files/links, accessibility, keyboard use, mobile readability, privacy, security, rights/attribution, examples, and maintenance needs. Report each finding with severity (blocker / fix soon / optional), exact evidence, likely recipient impact, and smallest remedy. Distinguish what you inspected from what you could not run or verify. Do not rewrite the product or mark it approved until I choose which changes to accept. Give a release checklist with observable pass/fail checks.
```

## 8. Learn from a release without over-measuring

```text
Here is what we actually released and the optional feedback we received: [facts and anonymized feedback]. Group observations by recipient task, quote only text I have permission to share, and separate evidence from interpretation. Identify the top three points of friction and one small improvement for each. Note contradictory or missing evidence. Propose a next-step priority based on recipient benefit, effort, and risk—not vanity metrics. Do not infer demographics, claim causation, expose identifying details, or suggest hidden tracking. Keep unrelated roadmap ideas in a later list.
```

## Quick customization

For a Luna-style early-supporter gift, add verified context such as “No signup; the seven browser helpers run locally; this pack includes a beginner project and 20 worked prompts.” Remove any detail that is not true for the freebie you are making.
